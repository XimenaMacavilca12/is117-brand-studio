IS117-007 / Research draft

Consistency / Research record

Same shirt.
Different meanings.

How do three clothing brands use visual style, brand character, and persuasion to give a plain white T-shirt a different meaning?

Working draft. This comparison uses Zara, Uniqlo, and Target product pages viewed on November 16, 2026. Peer review is still pending.

01 Sources02 Positions03 Audience04 Decisions
01 / Observe

Inspect the original.

The sources below show how each company presents its white T-shirt. Product claims come from the companies.

Zara / Original source inspected

A styled wardrobe piece

Zara: BASIC HEAVYWEIGHT T-SHIRT /03, White
Accessed November 16, 2026 · United States site

Inspect the original product photograph

Observed evidence
The inspected image shows a model wearing the white tee with loose jeans, glasses, and pink shoes against a plain wall. The page briefly describes regular fit, dense cotton, and a heavier weight than its medium-weight basic. It links measurements and care.
What it suggests
The outfit makes the shirt feel like part of a complete look. The simple photo and short description give it a stylish, understated feel.
Strength and drawback
Good for outfit inspiration, but it explains less about the shirt’s construction than Uniqlo.
Idea for Consistency
Show an everyday outfit and briefly explain how the shirt fits the look.

Uniqlo / Original source inspected

Explain the everyday essential

Uniqlo: SUPIMA® Cotton T-Shirt, 00 White
Accessed November 16, 2026 · United States site

Inspect the original product photograph

Observed evidence
The page describes cotton fibers, texture, collar stitching, and adjustments to neckline, body width, and sleeve length. The white-tee image shows the shirt on a model; a linked fabric close-up uses another color. Size options and a bestseller label are present.
What it suggests
The details give shoppers reasons to choose a basic shirt. The brand feels practical and polished. Fabric quality has not been independently tested here.
Strength and drawback
The clearest fit for Consistency’s everyday audience. Helpful details may make choosing easier, although some shoppers may prefer less text.
Idea for Consistency
Explain useful details in plain language. Start with styling advice; add fabric and fit claims only when there is evidence for them.

Target / Original source inspected

Reassure the practical shopper

Target: Goodfellow & Co Every Wear Short Sleeve 100% Cotton T-Shirt, White S
Accessed November 16, 2026 · United States site

Inspect the original product photograph

Observed evidence
The inspected page shows a $10 price, star rating with a review count, Q&A, size guide, fit bullets, and purchase controls. Its photograph shows a model in a plain white tee and dark trousers against a neutral background. Copy emphasizes comfort and everyday use.
What it suggests
Price, reviews, and size information make the shirt feel like a practical everyday purchase. Ratings offer social proof, while Q&A helps answer shopper concerns.
Strength and drawback
Useful for shoppers who care about price and reassurance. The extra information can also distract from the shirt itself.
Idea for Consistency
Keep price and size guidance easy to find. Target’s page calls the fabric both midweight and lightweight, showing why details need to be checked. Use reviews only when they are real.

What was actually inspected

This compares US product pages and photographs viewed on November 16, 2026. The shirts differ in fabric, fit, and price. The comparison covers these pages, rather than every ad from each company.

Prices and page content can change. No sales data, customer study, or product testing was available to prove which approach works best.

02 / Compare

Two possible positions.

The research suggests two possible directions for Consistency.

Everyday simplicity

Present the shirt as an easy everyday choice, using clear details like Uniqlo and practical information like Target.

Question: What makes the basic feel useful rather than anonymous?

Expressive styling

Present the shirt as a starting point for personal style. Zara’s outfit photo shows how other clothes can give a plain tee character.

Question: Does the styling help visitors imagine their own outfit?

Everyday simplicity fits the current audience more closely. Styling examples could also help people who want a more expressive outfit.

03 / Hypothesize

Who is it for?

These possible customers come from the existing brand plan. They are assumptions, not interview findings.

Current primary / Existing choice

The everyday wearer

Situation: Choosing an outfit for an ordinary day, coffee, or a date.

Need: Fashionable, plain, solid-color clothing without words or bold statements.

Concern: Clothing drawing attention away from the wearer.

Page task: Help them decide whether the tee suits their everyday style.

Secondary / Incomplete hypothesis

The expressive dresser

Need from the existing record: A simple tee that can feel more expressive.

Situation and concern: Not chosen yet.

Possible situation: Someone putting together a layered outfit may want styling examples. This still needs to be confirmed.

Current audience: The everyday wearer remains the focus. Clear explanations and simple outfit ideas suit this audience. Any change to the audience is still undecided. Inspect the existing brand decisions.

Comparison / Findings

Which seems most effective?

The comparison considers audience fit, clear reasons to choose, and help with shopping questions. It does not measure sales.

Uniqlo: strongest fit for the current audience

Uniqlo seems most effective for Consistency’s everyday audience because it explains what makes a simple shirt worth choosing. Zara offers more outfit inspiration, while Target offers more shopping reassurance. This is a comparison of the pages, not proof of better sales.

  • Zara: strongest outfit inspiration in the inspected photographs; a full look makes the shirt part of personal style.
  • Uniqlo: strongest explanation of construction and everyday versatility in the inspected copy.
  • Target: strongest purchase reassurance in the inspected text through price, ratings, Q&A, and fit information.

What would establish effectiveness? A fair comparison would test the same audience and offer. Customer ratings and bestseller labels alone cannot prove that an ad works better.

04 / Trace

Evidence into a decision.

The source findings lead to a small idea for the site and a way to check it.

Proposed decision / Explain one everyday styling choice

  1. Evidence: Uniqlo’s product page explains construction choices; Zara’s example shows the tee within a full outfit. Both were inspected on November 16, 2026.
  2. Inference: A short explanation and outfit example may help shoppers picture wearing the tee. Some visitors may care more about price.
  3. Proposal: Keep the simple design and add a small outfit illustration beside the existing styling tip. This is a proposed change, not a finished feature.
  4. Alternative: A large outfit photo could help with styling, but it would take more space. A review section would need real customer feedback.
  5. Planned check: Show a classmate versions with and without the illustration, keeping the other content the same. Ask who the page is for, what outfit it suggests, and where to choose a size. Record their feedback.
  6. Observed result: Not tested yet. Classmate feedback can guide a revision, but cannot prove better sales.

Honest persuasion

Keep the free styling tip and familiar everyday examples. Any reviews, product claims, or limited-stock messages need real evidence.
